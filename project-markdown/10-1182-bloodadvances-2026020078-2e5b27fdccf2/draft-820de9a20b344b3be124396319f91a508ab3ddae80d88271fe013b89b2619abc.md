TO THE EDITOR:

Global validation of revised sFLC cutoffs in amyloidosis

Sigurður Yngvi Kristinsson 1 and Þ ´ orir Einarsson Long 2

1 Department of Hematology, Faculty of Medicine, University of Iceland, Reykjavík, Iceland; and 2 Department of Hematology, Skåne University Hospital, Lund, Sweden

We read with great interest the American Society of Hematology 2026 guidelines on the diagnosis of light chain (AL) amyloidosis. 1 These recommendations are timely and essential for early recognition and avoiding unnecessary procedures.

We wish to provide recent evidence that further informs interpretation of serum-free light chain (sFLC) measurements.

Although the guidelines appropriately note potential limitations in generalizability of the iStopMM reference intervals, external validation has since been a priority. The iStopMM sFLC framework has now been evaluated across multiple non-Icelandic, ethnically diverse cohorts. Following our report on kidney-specific sFLC reference intervals, we also established age-stratified 99% reference intervals in individuals with normal kidney function, leading to a revised definition of light chain monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance. 2

In \>75 000 screened individuals, accounting for age-related increases in sFLC levels reduced light chain monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance overdiagnosis by 82%. Notably, among 1006 individuals reclassified as normal, none progressed to lymphoproliferative disorders over 4648 patient-years of follow-up (see table).

Beyond Iceland, the iStopMM sFLC framework has been independently validated across globally distributed, racially heterogeneous populations, including nationwide Scandinavian cohorts, 3 a large real-world Israeli cohort, 4 a Middle Eastern reference interval study, 5 a multicenter Chinese chronic kidney disease cohort, 6 and supportive data from racially diverse populations including African American individuals and cohorts from South Africa. 7

Diagnostic safety and accuracy have also been demonstrated specifically in amyloidosis. 8 In an Italian cohort of 1705 patients with AL amyloidosis and 675 with wild-type transthyretin amyloidosis, Milani et al 8 showed that the revised sFLC ratio preserved diagnostic sensitivity for AL amyloidosis, could be incorporated into complete response criteria, and substantially reduced isolated sFLC ratio abnor-malities in suspected wild-type transthyretin amyloidosis, thereby limiting the need for cardiac biopsy.

Taken together, these data support the generalizability, clinical safety, and disease-specific validity of kidney- and age-aware sFLC reference intervals, directly addressing the uncertainty around sFLC ratio cutoffs acknowledged by the guideline panel.
