Identifying High-Risk Smoldering Multiple Myeloma for Early Intervention

Cecilie V. Maeng, MD; Sæmundur Rögnvaldsson, MD, PhD; Christian Brieghel, MD, PhD; Morten N. Andersen, MD, PhD; Ida B. Kristensen, MD, PhD; Agoston Szabo, MD, DMSc; Kirsten Grønbæk, MD, DMSc; Carsten U. Niemann, MD, PhD; Sigurdur Y. Kristinsson, MD, PhD; Sigrún Thorsteinsdóttir, MD, PhD

IMPORTANCE Early treatment may benefit patients with high-risk smoldering multiple myeloma (SMM), a precursor of active multiple myeloma, but inconsistent high-risk definitions may capture different disease subsets.

OBJECTIVE To compare 2 high-risk SMM risk definitions: the AQUILA trial inclusion criteria and the 2/20/20 risk stratification model.

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS This cohort study included data from 2 separate SMM cohorts and a retrospective analysis assessing progression risk. The Iceland Screens, Treats, and Prevents Multiple Myeloma (iStopMM) is a large population-based screening study in Iceland, conducted from 2016 to 2021, while the Danish Lymphoid Cancer Resource (DALY-CARE) is a nationwide clinical data collection from Denmark from 2002 to 2025. From the iStopMM cohort, individuals with SMM were detected by screening. In DALY-CARE, individuals with SMM were identified in a clinical setting. Data were analyzed from March 2025 to January 2026.

EXPOSURE High-risk SMM by the AQUILA criteria, defined by presence of immunoparesis, monoclonal protein (M-protein) greater than 3.0 g/dL, immunoglobin A isotype, bone marrow plasma cell infiltration greater than 50%, or free light chain ratio 8 or greater. High risk by the 2/20/20 model was defined by presence of more than 1 of: M-protein greater than 2.0 g/dL, bone marrow plasma cell infiltration greater than 20%, or free light chain ratio greater than 20.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES Proportion of high-risk SMM in the 2 cohorts by both models and risk of progression as defined by initiation of treatment.

RESULTS A total of 193 individuals (median \[IQR\] age, 70 \[63-72\] years; 116 \[60%\] female) from the iStopMM cohort and 1147 individuals (median \[IQR\] age, 72 \[64-79\] years; 598 \[52%\] female) from the DALY-CARE cohort were included. In the screened cohort (iStopMM), 65 participants (34%) had high-risk SMM according to the AQUILA criteria, and 15 participants (8%) had high-risk SMM according to the 2/20/20 model. In the clinical cohort (DALY-CARE), 607 participants (55%) met high-risk criteria according to AQUILA, and 219 participants (19%) had high-risk SMM according to the 2/20/20 model. For individuals with AQUILA-defined high-risk disease in the clinical cohort, the 2-year progression risk was 27.0% (95% CI, 23.3%-30.7%), with an annual progression rate of 14.5%. For individuals with high risk defined by 2/20/20, the 2-year progression risk was 44.1% (95% CI, 37.1%-51.1%), with an annual progression rate of 27.3%.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE This cohort study found that the AQUILA trial criteria classified approximately 3 times more patients with SMM as having high risk compared with the 2/20/20 model and captured a group with substantially lower progression risk. These results suggest that the 2/20/20 model more accurately identifies a truly high-risk group with SMM, for whom early treatment is more likely to be beneficial.

moldering multiple myeloma (SMM) is an asymptom-atic precursor to multiple myeloma (MM), with a prevalence of 0.5% in individuals aged older than 40 years.1

The standard of care in SMM has been observation until progression, but recent studies suggest early treatment may benefit patients with high risk of progression.2-4 Most recently, the AQUILA trial evaluated daratumumab treatment compared with observation for high-risk SMM and found improve-ments in progression-free and overall survival.2 Other trials evaluating early treatment in SMM have used different definitions of high-risk SMM, which limits comparison.2-6 Since the initial design of the AQUILA trial, the Mayo Clinic 2/20/20 risk stratification model has become widely recognized and implemented in clinical settings.7,8 The choice of risk stratification model is crucial to identify individuals with truly high progression risk who may benefit from treatment, while avoiding overtreating individuals with lower risk. The primary objective of this study was to compare the distribution and progression risk of individuals with high-riskSMMbytheAQUILAtrialinclusioncriteriaandthe2/20/20 risk model in 2 SMM cohorts: a prospective nationwide screening cohort and a retrospectively identified population-based clinical cohort.

### Methods

The iStopMM study was approved by the Icelandic National BioethicsCommitteeandtheIcelandicDataProtectionAgency, and all participants provided informed consent. The DALY-CARE study was approved by the Danish Health Data Author-ity and National Ethics Committee. Under Danish legislation, register studies are exempt from informed consent. This study is reported following the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline. The Iceland Screens, Treats, or Prevents Multiple Myeloma (iStopMM) cohort is derived from population-based screening of 75 422 individuals in Iceland by serum protein electrophoresis and free light chain (FLC) assay and a subse-quent randomized study of follow-up strategies from 2016 to 2021.9 The Danish Lymphoid Cancer Research (DALY-CARE) data resource10 is a clinical nationwide data collection of more than 75 000 patients in Denmark with lymphoid-lineage cancers or precursors, linking databases and national registries, including the Danish Multiple Myeloma Registry, from 2002 to 2025.11

The iStopMM study design has been previously de-scribed in detail.1 In DALY-CARE, SMM was defined as an International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) MM diagnosis in individuals who neither died nor received antimyeloma therapy within 90 days from the date of diagnosis. ### Discussion

In this cohort study including data from both a screened cohort and clinical SMM cohort, the group with high-risk SMM as defined by the AQUILA trial inclusion criteria was larger (34% and 55%, respectively) than the high-risk group according to the 2/20/20 model (8% and 19%, respectively). Furthermore, the high-risk group defined by the AQUILA criteria had only a 27% progression risk within 2 years, compared with 44% for the 2/20/20 model. These findings demonstrate that the choice of risk stratification method markedly affects the number of patients in consideration for early treatment in SMM. Inaccurate risk assessment may increase the risk of overtreatment and overdiagnosis, bur-dening both the individual and the health care system. To balance these factors, a high risk of progression (approximately 50% at 2 years) is essential to define high-risk SMM,13,14 leaving the 2/20/20 model as the better option.

Limitations This study has some limitations. This study includes 2 well-characterized SMM cohorts: a screened cohort and a clinical cohort, each with different limitations, often complemented in the other. The iStopMM cohort is smaller but thoroughly annotated, with minimal missing data and selection bias. The DALY-CARE cohort is larger, but individuals may have been excluded or misclassified due to missing data.8 The 90-day re-striction (justified in eMethods in Supplement 1) may intro-duce immortal time bias, but importantly, sensitivity land-mark analyses demonstrated consistent results. Furthermore, cytogenetic data were unavailable, and most individuals in both cohorts were of Northern European descent, which may limit generalizability.

### Conclusions

This cohort study found that in both a screened and a clinical SMM cohort, the choice of SMM risk stratification model substantially determined how many individuals were classified as having high-risk disease. The AQUILA trial inclusion criteria incorporate a larger fraction of patients as having high risk of progression than the 2/20/20 model, with a markedly lower risk of progression. As treatment of high-risk SMM moves into clinicalpractice,usingthe2/20/20criteriaisthecurrentlymost appropriate method to define this population, minimizing overtreatment and improving the overall risk-benefit balance, leading to better outcomes for patients.
