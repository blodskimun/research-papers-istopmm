### ABSTRACT

BACKGROUND Detection of monoclonal components (MCs) using serum and urine immunofixation (IFE) and free light

chain measurement is a critical early step in diagnosing cardiac amyloidosis. Patients with MCs are referred for biopsy-

based diagnostic work-up. New reference ranges for the free light chain ratio (FLCR), adjusted for age and estimated

glomerular filtration rate, have been proposed.

OBJECTIVES The aim of this study was to compare the diagnostic performance of the new vs the conventional FLCR in

patients with light chain (AL) amyloidosis and wild-type transthyretin (ATTRwt) amyloidosis.

METHODS The analysis included 1,705 patients with AL amyloidosis and 675 with ATTRwt amyloidosis.

RESULTS In the AL cohort, 44 patients (3%) had negative results on serum and urine IFE at diagnosis. Among these,

13 patients had normal conventional FLCRs and 15 had normal new FLCRs, with no significant difference in diagnostic

sensitivity (70.4% \[95% CI: 55.8%-82.5%\] vs 65.9% \[95% CI: 50.0%-79.5%\]; P = 0.82). Overall diagnostic sensitivity

for MC detection was similar between the new and conventional FLCRs (99.2% vs 99.1%). Among patients with ATTRwt

amyloidosis and negative serum and urine IFE results, 156 (26.5%) had abnormal FLCRs using the conventional cutoff,

compared with only 12 (2%) using the new FLCR. At hematologic response assessment in AL amyloidosis, concordance in

the definition of complete response between the 2 FLCR methods was 94.9% (95% CI: 93.4%-95.9%).

CONCLUSIONS The new FLCR demonstrates equivalent diagnostic sensitivity in AL amyloidosis and can be integrated

into complete response criteria. In patients with suspected ATTRwt amyloidosis, it significantly reduces the proportion

of FLCR-only abnormalities, thereby limiting the need for biopsy confirmation. (JACC CardioOncol. 2025;7:751–759)

a critical first step in the diagnostic

work-up of systemic amyloidoses. 1 The

highest diagnostic sensitivity for detecting

monoclonal proteins is achieved by

combining serum and urine immunofixation

(IFE) with serum free light chain (sFLC) mea-

surement. 2-7 Furthermore, the free light

chain ratio (FLCR) is incorporated into the

definition of hematologic complete response

following chemotherapy in light chain (AL)

sFLC = serum free light chain

The first sFLC assay was introduced in

2001 14 and is recommended by the Interna-

tional Myeloma Working Group as a complementary

test to serum protein electrophoresis and IFE in the

evaluation of plasma cell dyscrasia. 15 An abnormal

FLCR serves as a sensitive numerical indicator of

clonality. 14 More recently, the iStopMM (Iceland

Screens Treats or Prevents Multiple Myeloma) study

proposed new FLCR ranges on the basis of age

(\<70 years vs $70 years) and estimated glomerular

filtration rate (eGFR; \<60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 vs

$60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 ). 16-18 However, these revised

reference intervals have not yet been evaluated in

patients with AL amyloidosis.

Recognition of wild-type transthyretin (ATTRwt)

amyloidosis with cardiac involvement has rapidly

increased in recent years, largely because of the

adoption of a nonbiopsy diagnostic approach based

on cardiac uptake of bone tracers in patients without

detectable monoclonal proteins. 19,20 The original

sFLC reference range proposed by the manufacturer

is 0.26 to 1.65. 3 However, it is now well established

that kidney function and age influence sFLC

concentrations, limiting the applicability of this

standard range, particularly in patients with ATTRwt

amyloidosis, who typically have a median age

\>75 years.

Several studies have applied alternative sFLC

reference intervals in the setting of ATTRwt

amyloidosis. 21-23 In patients with kidney dysfunc-

tion, either the modified FLCR introduced by

Hutchison et al 24 or the new reference intervals from

the iStopMM study 17 have been applied. However,

these studies did not consider the updated free light

chain (FLC) ranges designed for individuals with

eGFRs $60 mL/min/1.73 m 2 . 18

The aims of this study were 3-fold: 1) to compare

the diagnostic sensitivity of monoclonal protein

detection using the newly proposed FLC intervals

compared with the conventional FLCR in a consecu-

tive cohort of patients with AL amyloidosis; 2) to

assess the impact of the new FLCR on the definition

of hematologic complete response; and 3) to deter-

mine whether the new FLCR can reduce the need for

biopsy-proven diagnosis in a consecutive cohort of

patients with ATTRwt amyloidosis.

### METHODS

STUDY POPULATION. This study population included

2 cohorts of patients diagnosed and treated at the

Amyloidosis Research and Treatment Center in Pavia,

Italy. The AL amyloidosis cohort comprised newly

diagnosed patients evaluated between 2004 and

2021\. The ATTRwt amyloidosis cohort included

newly diagnosed patients evaluated between 2006

and 2021. Patients in both cohorts were prospectively

followed for overall survival.

Data were obtained from 2 prospective studies

approved by the local ethics committee: Re-AL

(A Registry of AL Amyloidosis; NCT04839003) and

the ITA-WebNet (Italian Transthyretin Amyloidosis

Web-Network; NCT05444920) study, both conducted

by Foundation IRCCS Policlinico San Matteo in Pavia.

All patients provided written informed consent for

use of their clinical data for research purposes.

DIAGNOSIS OF AL AND ATTRwt AMYLOIDOSIS. All

patients in the AL cohort had biopsy-proven di-

agnoses, with amyloid deposits confirmed as AL type

by immunoelectron microscopy and/or mass spec-

trometric analysis. 25-27 Patients with symptomatic

multiple myeloma, as defined by the International

Myeloma Working Group criteria, were excluded 28

because of the distinct testing panel recommended

for monoclonal protein detection in this setting. 29

In the ATTRwt cohort, diagnosis was established

according to consensus criteria. 20 In particular, a

nonbiopsy diagnosis was accepted in patients with

echocardiographic or cardiac magnetic resonance

findings suggestive of amyloid deposition, along with

cardiac uptake on 99m Tc 3,3-diphosphono-1,2-

propanodicarboxylic acid or 99m Tc hydroxy-

methylene diphosphonate scintigraphy (Perugini

grade 2 or 3), in the absence of monoclonal pro-

teins. 20 Monoclonal components were excluded by

negative serum and urine protein electrophoresis

with IFE and a normal serum FLCR, using the refer-

ence range defined by Katzmann et al 3 (0.26–1.65).

All patients with clinical and/or imaging findings

suggestive of cardiac amyloidosis and a concomitant

monoclonal protein underwent endomyocardial bi-

opsy if less invasive procedures (eg, fat pad aspirate

or minor salivary gland biopsy) produced negative

results. 30 All biopsies were stained with Congo red,

and transthyretin deposits were typed by

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS. Continuous variables are

reported as median (Q1-Q3) and categorical variables

as count (percentage). Diagnostic sensitivity, defined

as the ability of the new FLCR method to correctly

identify individuals with the disease (true-positive

rate), is presented as percentages with 95% CIs.

Concordance (agreement) is also reported as per-

centage with 95% CIs. Sensitivity between FLCR

methods was compared using the McNemar test for

paired data.

Statistical analyses were performed using MedCalc

version 22.016 (MedCalc Software). P values of \<0.05

were considered to indicate statistical significance.

The primary analysis focused on patients with AL

amyloidosis and negative serum and urine IFE

results.

### RESULTS

The AL cohort included 1,705 consecutive patients,

and the ATTRwt cohort included 675 patients. All

patients were diagnosed and followed at the Pavia

Amyloidosis Research and Treatment Center. Key

clinical and laboratory characteristics of both cohorts

are reported in Tables 2 and 3.

AL AMYLOIDOSIS: PATIENTS WITH NEGATIVE

SERUM AND URINE IFE AT DIAGNOSIS. In the AL

cohort, 44 patients (3%) had negative serum and

urine IFE results at diagnosis. Among these, 15 had

normal FLCRs using the new reference range, and 13

had normal ratios using the conventional FLCR. The

diagnostic sensitivity of the conventional FLCR was

70.4% (95% CI: 55.8%-82.5%), compared with 65.9%

(95% CI: 50.0%-79.5%) using the new FLCR; howev-

er, the difference was not statistically significant

(P = 0.50) (Central Illustration).

DIAGNOSTIC SENSITIVITY AND FLCR CONCOR-

DANCE IN THE ENTIRE AL COHORT. In the entire AL

cohort, concordance between the conventional and

new FLCR reference ranges, adjusted for age and

eGFR, is reported in Supplemental Table 1. Diag-

nostic sensitivity for monoclonal protein detection

in all 1,705 patients is shown in Table 4. Specif-

ically, the sensitivity of serum and urine IFE com-

bined with the conventional FLCR reference range

was 99.2%, compared with 99.1% using the

new FLCR.

At 6 months after treatment initiation, hemato-

logic response data were available for 1,168 patients

(68.5%), and 537 (31.5%) died before a second eval-

uation at our center. Concordance for FLCR normal-

ization after therapy using the 2 reference ranges was

80.3% (95% CI: 78.0%-82.5%). Concordance for he-

matologic complete response, defined by negative

serum and urine IFE results and a normal FLCR

using either reference range, was 94.9% (95% CI:

93.4%-95.9%).

MONOCLONAL PROTEIN DETECTION IN THE ATTRwt

COHORT WITH CARDIAC INVOLVEMENT. In the

ATTRwt cohort, 86 patients (12.7%) had detectable

monoclonal proteins by serum and/or urine IFE. The

FLCR was abnormal in 37 patients (5.4%) using the

conventional reference range and in 17 patients

(2.5%) using the new FLCR range.

Bone marrow biopsy was performed in 38 patients

at the discretion of the local physician. Among these,

only 7 patients were diagnosed with smoldering

multiple myeloma, defined by bone marrow plasma

cell infiltrate \> 10%. In all 7 cases, the FLCR was

abnormal using both reference ranges. No bone le-

none of the patients had laboratory findings consis-

tent with symptomatic multiple myeloma, as defined

by Rajkumar et al. 28

No patients with negative serum and urine IFE

results had FLCRs less than the reference range using

either the conventional or new definitions. However,

156 patients (26.5%) had elevated FLCRs using the

conventional reference range, compared with only 12

patients (2%) using the new FLCR reference ranges

(Figure 1, Table 5). Concordance between the con-

ventional and new FLCR reference ranges, adjusted

for age and eGFR, is further detailed in Supplemental

Table 1. All patients with abnormal FLCRs and nega-

tive serum and urine IFE required a biopsy-proven

approach.

Overall, 242 patients (35.8%) were classified as

having diagnosed or suspected monoclonal proteins

using the combination of serum and/or urine IFE and

the conventional FLCR, compared with 98 patients

(14.4%) using the new FLCR (P \< 0.001). None of the

patients with ATTRwt amyloidosis and monoclonal

proteins progressed to symptomatic multiple

myeloma during a median follow-up of 34 months

(Q1-Q3: 23-50 months) among living patients.

### DISCUSSION

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to

demonstrate that 1) the newly proposed FLCR by the

iStopMM investigators can be applied in the diag-

nosis of AL amyloidosis with high diagnostic sensi-

tivity; 2) the new FLCR can be integrated into

hematologic complete response criteria in AL

amyloidosis; and 3) in patients with suspected

ATTRwt amyloidosis, use of the new FLCR substan-

tially reduces the proportion of patients with isolated

FLCR abnormalities, thereby limiting the need for

invasive diagnostic testing.

In our AL cohort, the monoclonal protein was not

identified in fewer than 1% of patients: 13 using the

conventional FLCR and 15 using the new FLCR

reference intervals. Among patients with negative

serum and urine IFE, there was no significant dif-

ference in diagnostic sensitivity for detecting the

amyloidogenic light chain between the 2 FLCR defi-

nitions. Although this subgroup represented only 3%

of the total AL cohort, it remains critical to maintain a

high index of suspicion to avoid missed diagnoses.

Multiorgan involvement may serve as a key clinical

clue suggestive of systemic AL amyloidosis.

Emerging high-sensitivity techniques, such as

mass spectrometry for monoclonal plasma protein

detection in serum and urine or the identification of

clonal plasma cells in the bone marrow, may enhance

diagnostic accuracy in these cases. 39-44

In the entire AL cohort, the new FLCR reference

range showed lower diagnostic sensitivity for κ light

chains compared with the conventional sFLC refer-

ence range, while sensitivity for the λ isotype was

higher. Most patients not identified by sFLC assay

using either reference interval were detected by

serum and/or urine IFE. These findings are consistent

with prior studies reporting optimal diagnostic

sensitivity when combining serum and urine IFE with

sFLC measurement in AL amyloidosis. 2,4

STUDY LIMITATIONS. This study was retrospective,

was conducted at a single center, and included a

population with limited ethnic diversity. In addition,

we did not compare different FLC assays. Further

prospective studies with internal testing and external

validation cohorts are needed to confirm these

results.

### CONCLUSIONS

In 2 large cohorts of patients with confirmed AL and

ATTRwt amyloidosis, the application of the newly

proposed FLCR reference intervals demonstrated

excellent overall diagnostic accuracy for identifying

AL amyloidosis and evaluating hematologic complete

response. Furthermore, in patients with ATTRwt

amyloidosis, the new FLCR improved specificity and

may reduce the number of unnecessary biopsies.

These findings may support revisions of diagnostic

algorithms. Further studies are warranted to assess

clinical implementation of newer, more sensitive

methods for detecting monoclonal proteins, partic-

ularly in patients with negative serum and urine IFE

results.

PERSPECTIVES

COMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: The

identification of monoclonal light chains is a critical

decision point in the diagnostic work-up of systemic

amyloidoses, directing patients with monoclonal

proteins toward a biopsy-based evaluation. This study

shows that the newly proposed FLC reference inter-

vals, when used in combination with IFE, maintain

diagnostic sensitivity for AL amyloidosis and may

reduce the need for invasive diagnostic procedures in

patients with ATTRwt amyloidosis.

TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: The adoption of the

new FLCR may significantly reduce the need for

biopsy-based diagnosis in patients with suspected

ATTRwt amyloidosis. Further research is needed to

assess the role of other sensitive methods, such as

mass spectrometry, for the detection of monoclonal

proteins in this context.
